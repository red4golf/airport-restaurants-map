Airport Restaurants — Fly-In Dining Guide for Pilots

Airport Restaurants is a directory of fly-in dining destinations at general aviation airports across the United States. Browse on-field restaurants, classic $100 hamburger stops, and the best fly-in meals by state and region.

Featured Fly-In Restaurants by State

  • Jefferson County Intl (0S9) — Port Townsend, WA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Spruce Goose Café — On-airport. A legendary fly-in destination since the 1960s. Aviation-themed décor and homemade pies — marionberry and rhubarb are the ones to get. Named a Top 10 Airport Restaurant by AOPA. Watch planes taxi past while you eat. Wed–Sun 8AM–4PM.

  • Bremerton National Airport (KPWT) — Bremerton, WA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Amelia's Hangar Restaurant and Lounge — On-airport. Amelia Earhart-themed restaurant inside a plane hangar at Bremerton National. American classics with generous portions. Sunday brunch buffet draws pilots and locals alike. Tue–Thu 6AM–8PM · Fri–Sat 6AM–9PM · Sun 6AM–8PM · Closed Mon.

  • Tacoma Narrows Airport (KTIW) — Gig Harbor, WA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • The Hub - Gig Harbor — On-airport. On-field restaurant right at Tacoma Narrows with runway views. Burgers, pizza, Cajun tots and Harmon craft beers on tap. Watch GA aircraft and business jets roll by. Open daily 11AM–9PM.

  • Gaston's Airport (3M0) — Lakeview, AR

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Gaston's Restaurant — On-airport. World-famous fly-in fishing resort on the White River. The restaurant serves fresh trout caught steps from the table. One of the most unique fly-in dining experiences in the country — land on the private strip and walk to the table.

  • Chino Airport (KCNO) — Chino, CA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Flo's Airport Café — On-airport. Classic airport diner at Chino Airport — home of the Planes of Fame Air Museum. Breakfast and lunch staples served with a side of warbird sightings. A longtime favorite for pilots flying the Southern California basin. Sources disagree on hours; the operator's own ordering page says Tue–Sun 6AM–3PM. Call (909) 597-3416.

  • Harris Ranch Airport (3O8) — Coalinga, CA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Harris Ranch Restaurant — On-airport. A legendary California pit stop on the I-5 corridor. Land at the private Harris Ranch strip and walk to one of the state's most famous steakhouses. Prime beef raised on-site. Open for breakfast, lunch and dinner daily.

  • Camarillo Airport (KCMA) — Camarillo, CA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Waypoint Cafe — On-airport. Popular on-field café at Camarillo Airport serving breakfast and lunch. Generous portions and a friendly pilot crowd. On clear days the Channel Islands are visible from the ramp. Open daily 7AM–3PM.

  • Centennial Airport (KAPA) — Englewood, CO

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • The Perfect Landing — On-airport. Upscale dining with panoramic views of the Front Range and runway at Centennial Airport. One of Colorado's best airport restaurants — think steaks and Colorado craft beers with a view of the Rockies. Open for lunch and dinner.

  • Waterbury-Oxford Airport (KOXC) — Oxford, CT

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Volo — On-airport. Opened in the former Restaurant 121 space, which their own site puts about 300 feet from the runway, with floor-to-ceiling glass and both indoor and patio seating. It is run by an in-flight catering company that moved its operation into the building, so the kitchen's aviation credentials are literal. Casual fine dining: elevated American plates plus Neapolitan pizza. Watch the schedule — lunch Tue–Sun 11AM–3PM, dinner Thu–Sat only 5PM–9:30PM, closed Mondays. Reservations suggested for parties of five or more. (203) 266-2107.

  • Spruce Creek Fly-In (7FL6) — Port Orange, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Fly In Cafe — Near airport. Opened September 2023 in the old Downwind Café space, keeping the cozy aviation-themed room and leaning into it on the menu — the Fly-In Omelet, the Spruce Creek Scrambler, and pizzas named The Ranch Runway and The Margherita Maneuver. Full bar, happy hour 3–7PM, live music Fri and Sat nights. Read this before you go: this is a drive-to destination. The restaurant's own access page states that 7FL6 "is a private airport with a strict INVITEE POLICY, which does not permit any access using the runway to visit the Cafe," and warns that unauthorized runway use or misstating your destination "may result in a trespass." Databases will mislead you here: the FAA record reads private-use PPR and AirNav even lists a POA number, but only a Spruce Creek resident can grant permission to land, the invitation must be specific to you, and the restaurant cannot issue one. You drive in through one of two guarded gates and present a license, which is copied. Breakfast Tue–Sat 8–11AM, Sun 8AM–12PM · Lunch Tue–Sat 11:30AM–4PM, Sun 12–4PM · Dinner Sun and Tue–Thu 4–8PM · Closed Mon.

  • Vero Beach Regional Airport (KVRB) — Vero Beach, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • CJ Cannon's — On-airport. A Vero Beach institution right on the airport with sweeping runway views. Known for generous portions of American comfort food and a lively bar. Popular with both Piper training fleet pilots from next door and transient GA traffic.

  • Winter Haven Regional Airport (KGIF) — Winter Haven, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Flightline Café — On-airport. Casual on-field diner at Winter Haven Regional serving breakfast and lunch. Friendly staff and a loyal local pilot following. Good stopping point between Tampa and Orlando in the heart of central Florida's lake country.

  • Bartow Municipal Airport (KBOW) — Bartow, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Tantrums Flightside Cafe — On-airport. A family-run operation inside the Bartow terminal building with ramp and runway views, serving Old Florida comfort food. Breakfast runs to the Pilot's Breakfast, chicken and waffles, and shrimp and grits; lunch to the Signature Smash Burger, a Cuban, Shrimp & Grit Cakes and gator bites. Burger Monday, Taco Tuesday and a Friday fish fry rotate through the week, and there is key lime pie waiting at the end of it. Mon–Sat 7AM–2PM · Closed Sun.

  • Venice Municipal Airport (KVNC) — Venice, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Suncoast Café — On-airport. Relaxed café at Venice Municipal — the shark tooth capital of the world. Breakfast and lunch with a friendly airport crowd. Venice has one of the most active GA communities on Florida's Gulf Coast.

  • Ocala Intl (Jim Taylor Field) (KOCF) — Ocala, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Elevation 89 — On-airport. The most polished restaurant on this map that still lets you park on the ramp and walk in. Opened 2021 in the center of the Sheltair terminal: floor-to-ceiling windows onto the runway, a bar built from an aircraft wing and equestrian touches for Marion County horse country. Crab cakes, porcini-dusted scallops, lobster spring rolls, and a serious burger list including the Flying Goat with goat cheese and fig jam. Sun–Thu 11AM–9PM · Fri–Sat 11AM–10PM. Reservations on OpenTable; sunset over the runway is the draw.

  • Albert Whitted Airport (KSPG) — St. Petersburg, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • The Hangar Restaurant & Flight Lounge — On-airport. Waterfront airport restaurant at Albert Whitted with stunning views of Tampa Bay and the St. Pete skyline. One of Florida's most scenic dining spots. Open for breakfast and lunch — arrive early on weekends.

  • Okeechobee County Airport (KOBE) — Okeechobee, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Landing Strip Café — On-airport. Down-home Florida diner at Okeechobee County Airport. Breakfast and lunch served to a mix of ag pilots, fishermen and cross-country traffic. Okeechobee sits at the north end of the lake — good stopping point for south Florida trips.

  • Fort Lauderdale Executive Airport (KFXE) — Fort Lauderdale, FL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Jet Runway Café — On-airport. Casual café at Fort Lauderdale Executive — one of the busiest GA airports in the country. Breakfast and lunch with a front-row seat to a parade of jets, turboprops and helicopters. Great people-watching between bites.

  • DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) — Atlanta, GA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • 57th Fighter Group Restaurant — Near airport. WWII-themed restaurant adjacent to DeKalb-Peachtree with a control tower you can dine in. Runway views, wartime memorabilia and a full menu of American classics. One of the most atmospheric airport-adjacent restaurants in the Southeast.

  • Schaumburg Regional Airport (06C) — Schaumburg, IL

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Pilot Pete's — On-airport. Beloved on-field diner at Schaumburg Regional in the Chicago suburbs. Big breakfasts, burgers and a loyal pilot crowd. One of the busiest small GA airports in Illinois — expect company on weekend mornings.

  • Eagle Creek Airpark (KEYE) — Indianapolis, IN

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Rick's Café Boatyard — Near airport. Waterfront restaurant on Eagle Creek Reservoir adjacent to Eagle Creek Airpark. Seafood, burgers and lake views. A fun combination of the boating and flying communities in one spot on the west side of Indianapolis.

  • Beaumont Hotel Airport (07S) — Beaumont, KS

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • The Beaumont Hotel Café — On-airport. Historic hotel café at a private Kansas airstrip. The Beaumont Hotel dates to 1879 and has hosted cattle drives and oil barons. A genuine step back in time — land at the grass strip and walk to the dining room.

  • Bowman Field (KLOU) — Louisville, KY

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Bistro Le Relais — On-airport. French bistro in the historic terminal at Bowman Field — one of the oldest operating airports in the US. White tablecloths and classic French cuisine next to a ramp full of vintage aircraft. A genuinely surprising find at a Kentucky GA airport.

  • Minute Man Air Field (6B6) — Stow, MA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Fourth & Field — On-airport. A family-run farm-to-table restaurant that took over Nancy's Air Field Café in September 2023, with about 70 seats indoors and a 75-seat patio looking out over the field. The kitchen sources from named local farms and twenty of its own garden beds; brunch is the move — corned beef hash, build-your-own omelettes, steak and cheese on an everything bagel. Wed–Fri lunch 11AM–3PM and dinner from 4PM · Sat brunch 9AM–3PM and dinner from 4PM · Sun brunch 9AM–3PM · Closed Mon–Tue. Note the field itself is on the market as of 2026, though the owners say it is business as usual.

  • Nantucket Memorial Airport (KACK) — Nantucket, MA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Crosswinds Restaurant — On-airport. On-field restaurant at Nantucket Memorial with views of one of the most scenic GA airports on the East Coast. Breakfast and lunch — expect a mix of island residents, ferry commuters and summer transient traffic from throughout the Northeast.

  • St. Paul Downtown Airport (KSTP) — St. Paul, MN

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Holman's Table — On-airport. Upscale farm-to-table dining in the historic Holman Field terminal building overlooking the Mississippi River and downtown St. Paul skyline. One of the most architecturally beautiful airport restaurants in the Midwest. Lunch and dinner.

  • Gilliam-McConnell Airfield (5NC3) — Carthage, NC

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Pik-N-Pig BBQ — On-airport. A genuine BBQ landmark accessible only by air or a bumpy dirt road. Fly into this grass strip in the North Carolina Sandhills and taxi up to a pulled pork sandwich that's worth the trip. A bucket-list fly-in for East Coast pilots. Thu–Sun only.

A breakfast-and-lunch operation rather than a dinner house. This is the listing on this map we are least sure of: the restaurant posts no hours or news anywhere we could find, and the most recent independent coverage we could turn up is from early 2025 — aggregators still show it active. Unrelated to Volo at Oxford, CT.

  • Greenville Downtown Airport (KGMU) — Greenville, SC

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

A 16-foot glass hangar door frames the runway, and there is a gated playground for anyone flying with kids. Currently Tue–Sun 11AM–3PM, closed Mon; dinner and weekend breakfast have been announced but are not yet running. The name honors two women born in 1928 and nods to the airport's 2028 centennial.

  • Independence State Airport (7S5) — Independence, OR

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Starduster Cafe — On-airport. About as short a walk as fly-in dining gets — the cafe sits on the east side of the runway, roughly 200 feet from the transient tie-downs. A straightforward family-run diner: eggs, hash browns, corned beef hash, biscuits and gravy, country fried steak, and window seats looking straight out at the pattern. Independence charges no landing or tie-down fees. Plan it as a morning flight — the kitchen closes at 2PM every day. Mon–Sat 6AM–2PM · Sun 7AM–2PM. Pattern note: gliders and ultralights fly RIGHT traffic for 16/34 while everyone else flies left — two patterns on opposite sides of the same runway at once. Expect ultralights on and around the field and waterfowl in season. CTAF 122.975.

  • Harvey Field (S43) — Snohomish, WA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Buzz Inn Steakhouse — On-airport. One of the few fly-in options in Puget Sound that will still feed you at eight in the evening — open seven days from 7AM for breakfast through dinner, which is unusual for an on-field restaurant. Hand-cut Angus steaks aged at least 28 days are the house specialty, with all-day breakfast, a full bar, and indoor and outdoor seating. Sun–Thu 7AM–9PM · Fri–Sat 7AM–10PM. Pattern note: Harvey is an active jump field and the parachute landing area is the gravel circle between the paved runway and the main taxiway — which is why 15L is RIGHT traffic and the field asks that all patterns stay west. A standard left pattern to 15L puts you over the jumpers. Second student drop zone 1nm east marked with a white X, helicopter training west of the runways at and below 500ft, no touch-and-goes. CTAF 123.0. The runway is 2,672 x 36 ft with a 452 ft displaced threshold on 15L — worth the numbers before a night arrival. The restaurant's lease runs through at least September 2027.

  • City of Colorado Springs Municipal Airport (KCOS) — Colorado Springs, CO

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • The Airplane Restaurant — Near airport. You eat inside a 1953 Boeing KC-97 tanker — 42 of the roughly 275 seats are in the airframe itself, and the cockpit is open to walk through. Diner-plus American: the Reuben von Crashed, the BBQ Bomber Burger, Runway Chicken Wings, Angus steaks. It is outside the airport fence, but the walk is genuinely easy: out the landside door at Cutter Aviation or jetCenters of Colorado and southwest to Newport Road, about half a mile and ten minutes on foot, no ramp crossing and no badging. Sun–Thu 11AM–8PM · Fri–Sat 11AM–9PM. Two flight-planning notes: the field sits at 6,187 ft and summer afternoon density altitude runs into five figures, and both FBOs are jet houses — ask about a ramp fee before you go. KCOS is Class C.

  • Pierce County Airport (Thun Field) (KPLU) — Puyallup, WA

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Top Gun Bar and Grill — On-airport. A working pilots' bar and grill right on the ramp at Thun Field, midfield on the west side with a straight view of the pattern. Burgers, club sandwiches, fish and chips, nachos and pizza, plus omelettes and skillets on the weekend breakfast menu. Free pool and cornhole on the back patio. Open seven days: Mon to Wed 11AM to midnight, Thu and Fri 11AM to 2AM, Sat 8AM to 2AM, Sun 8AM to midnight. (253) 848-0555. IMPORTANT: this venue is 21 and over only, no children. Field notes: runway 17/35 is 3,651 by 60 ft, narrower than most pilots are used to, with PAPI both ends on a steep 4.0 degree glidepath. Non-towered, CTAF 122.7, self-serve 100LL 24 hours. Noise sensitive in all quadrants, so fly the abatement procedures. Ultralights prohibited.

  • Ken Jernstedt Airfield (4S2) — Hood River, OR

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Twin Peak's Drive-In — Near airport. A classic roadside drive-in a quarter mile west of the field, sitting almost on the extended runway centerline. Handcrafted burgers with house-made sauces, four kinds of fries, and old-fashioned malted milkshakes. Pair it with the WAAAM antique aircraft and automobile museum on the field and Hood River becomes a day trip rather than a lunch stop. Open 10:30AM to 8:30PM. (541) 386-4460. Field notes: runway 7/25 is 3,040 by 75 ft asphalt, CTAF 122.8, 100LL available. Brief the Gorge before you go. The chart warns that crosswinds here produce extreme low level turbulence and unexpected wind currents, and it is the most important operational fact about this field. Glider operations are conducted here, and there is a grass median runway north of the pavement that cannot be used at the same time as the paved surface. Noise sensitive.

  • Cavanaugh Bay Airport (66S) — Coolin, ID

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Cavanaugh's — Near airport. Lakefront dining on Priest Lake about a tenth of a mile north of the parking apron, which is a two minute walk from the turf. The operator says plainly to fly in and join them. The menu leans regional: Priest Lake Pasta, fish tacos, steak salad, Selkirk nachos, and a huckleberry ice cream sandwich. Dining is first come first served with no reservations, though groups of ten or more are appreciated in advance. Summer hours Sun to Thu noon to 8PM, Fri and Sat noon to 9PM. No takeout on Friday or Saturday June through September. (208) 215-7088. Field notes: runway 15/33 is 3,100 by 120 ft turf, state owned by Idaho Transportation Department Aeronautics, elevation 2,484 ft. No fuel and no repairs, tiedowns only. There is no line of sight between the runway ends. Watch for irrigation sprinklers on the runway, and note the edges and thresholds are marked with white rocks. Seaplane operations in the adjacent bay, CTAF 122.9. Seasonal field with no winter maintenance.

  • Ravalli County Airport (HRF) — Hamilton, MT

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Hangar Cafe — On-airport. On the field at 303 Airport Rd, in a remodeled hangar looking straight at landing traffic. Owner operated for fifteen years by Ashley Hale, whose father converted the hangar into a cafe in 2005. Breakfast and lunch with aviation names on the menu including the Glider Omelet and the Spruce Goose Burger, and it was recently voted best breakfast in the Bitterroot. Fire season helicopter crews eat here, which tells you something about both the food and the turnaround. Wed to Fri 9AM to 2PM, Sat 8AM to 12:30PM, Sun 8AM to 1:30PM, closed Mon and Tue. (406) 363-4478. Field notes: the identifier changed from 6S5 to HRF in October 2021, so older guides and databases are wrong. Runway 17/35 is 5,200 by 75 ft asphalt with PAPI both ends, elevation 3,636 ft, 100LL and Jet-A on field. Parachute jump activity and ultralight activity are both noted here, so brief them.

  • Big Creek Airport (U60) — Big Creek, ID

    1 restaurant listed for pilots.

    • Big Creek Lodge — On-airport. The flagship backcountry meal in Idaho. The rebuilt lodge sits at the edge of the strip in the Frank Church River of No Return wilderness, running a short seasonal menu the lodge describes as healthy filling cuisine to fit backcountry tastes. Breakfast 7:30AM to 1PM at $35, lunch noon to 1PM at $35, dinner one seating at 6PM at $45. Breakfast needs no reservation and is the only walk-up meal. Dinner requires 48 hours notice and confirmation, and the lodge cannot take walk-in dinner guests because there is no phone on site and no grocery store within three hours. Closed Wednesdays during the 2026 season. (208) 949-1828. READ THIS FIRST: Idaho Division of Aeronautics states do not attempt operations at Big Creek without a solid fundamental background in mountain flying, and strongly recommends an airport checkout. Runway 01/19 is 3,550 by 110 ft turf at 5,743 ft elevation. Land runway 19 and depart runway 01. Straight-ins are strongly discouraged, and departing 19 southbound is strongly discouraged because of rising terrain and a history of high density altitude accidents. The first 400 ft of runway 01 is rough and undulating. Expect strong downdrafts on final, big game on and around the runway, trees 30 to 60 ft along the edges and ends, rodent holes and soft wet sections. No fuel, unattended, no winter maintenance.
